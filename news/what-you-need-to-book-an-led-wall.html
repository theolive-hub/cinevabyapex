      On set

      What we need to quote.

      By Cineva crew · June 9, 2026

      If a wall has landed on your plate to source, here is the short version: tell us the shot, the stage, and the shoot dates, and we can get you a real quote off one call. Everything below is what makes that quote fast and accurate instead of a week of back-and-forth.

      You do not need to speak LED to book one. You need to know what the scene is doing and when you are shooting. We turn that into pitch, wall size, processing, crew, and prep days. The more you can hand us up front, the less the shoot day has to improvise.

      What the shot needs

      The single most useful thing is what the wall is for. A driving scene, an environment the actors live in, a window of city light behind a desk, or a wall used purely as a soft source: each one builds differently. Tell us the shot type and we size the rest to it.

      Two details do most of the work. How close the camera gets to the wall sets the pixel pitch we spec, and how much the camera sees sets how big the wall and the coverage need to be. A reference frame or a storyboard helps more than a paragraph. If you are shooting more than one camera at once, flag it, because that is a frame remapping question.

      Where and when

      Where the wall goes matters as much as the wall. If you have a stage, tell us the room: ceiling height, power, and how a truck reaches the load-in door. If you do not have one, that is fine, because we bring the volume to your stage or location rather than booking you into ours. Either way we need the address and the access.

      Then the dates. The shooting days, and just as important, the prep runway before them. A wall is a build, not a drop-off, and a call at greenlight buys a calmer week than a call seven days out. Give us the shoot dates and we work the pre-light and load-in back from them.

      Who brings the world

      The wall needs something to show. For a driving scene you supply the plate, and if you need a starting point we point you to drivingplates.com. For a built environment, your virtual art department or content vendor makes the world, and we make sure it runs clean on the wall. If you are not sure what you need, tell us the look and we will tell you what to line up.

      It also helps to know your camera package and who your DP and DIT are, so colour and sync line up before the day rather than on it. None of this has to be locked to get a quote. Rough is fine. We firm it up together.

      How the call goes

      Practically, one call or email does it. Tell us the shot, the stage, the dates, and roughly the budget you are working to, and we come back with a scoped quote and a plan, not a catalogue. If a walkthrough makes sense, we book one. If green screen or a smaller build is the smarter spend, we will say so. Once it is booked, every order runs through a pre-flight pass before it ships.

      You do not have to have the answers to all of it. Send what you have. Tell us the shot and the dates, and the rest is our problem, not yours.

      Have a wall to source?

      Send the shot, the stage, and your dates. We come back with a scoped quote and a plan, and we flag anywhere green screen or a smaller build saves you money. No LED vocabulary required.
