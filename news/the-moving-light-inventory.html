      Specialty Lighting

      The moving-light inventory.

      By Cineva crew · June 19, 2026

      The fastest way to spec a moving-light package is to stop shopping by brand and start shopping by the job. A spot is a spot whether the badge on the yoke reads one name or another. Cineva by Apex Sound & Light runs a deep moving-light package for film, TV, and broadcast, and this is the map: what each fixture is for, and when to reach for it.

      We run the houses crews already trust. Robe carries the deepest part of the package, the broadcast and live workhorse line that holds up shoot after shoot. Ayrton brings the large-format output for when you need to throw light a long way and keep it clean. GLP covers the pixel bars and the effect work. Chauvet, Elation, and Acme round it out, from IP-rated fixtures that live outside to budget-honest movers when the spec calls for count over hero. That is the marquee, not the whole shelf. The package runs deeper than any list and it moves with the work, so the current line card is the live source, not a paragraph on a web page.

      Spot, beam, wash, effects

      Here is the part that does not expire. Every moving light is built to do one of a few jobs, and once you know the job, the fixture almost picks itself.

      A spot, or a profile, is your hard-edged tool. It throws a defined circle you can shape with shutters, push a gobo through for texture, and hold a crisp edge across the room. Reach for it when you need control: a shaft through a window, a pattern on a wall, a tight pool on a mark.

      A beam is the tight, parallel shaft you see cutting through haze. It is not for lighting a face, it is for the look, the aerial, the column of light that reads on camera as energy. Reach for it when the beam itself is the shot.

      A wash is the soft, broad field. No hard edge, just colour and coverage you can blend across a set or a cyc. Reach for it when you are painting the frame rather than pointing at something in it.

      Effects fixtures are the multi-cell bars, strips, and pixel heads that do movement and pattern in one unit. Reach for them when you want the rig itself to perform.

      Most of the package does not sit in just one box, and that is the point. A beam-wash does two jobs from one yoke; a wash with an effects engine gives you a soft field and a pixel chase without a second fixture. On a tight count and a tighter truck, those hybrids are what let a small rig cover a big board. Tell us the looks and we build the count around them, not the other way around.

      Built for the camera

      Touring-grade output is the easy part. Surviving the camera is the part that separates a film package from a concert one.

      A fixture that looks perfect to the eye can strobe, flicker, or shift colour the moment you put it behind a high frame rate or a calibrated pipeline. We spec the package for the camera first: flicker-free at the frame rates you actually shoot, colour accurate enough to sit next to skin tones, and dimming that stays clean from full down to a whisper. Touring-grade output, broadcast discipline.

      And it arrives ready to work. The package ships patched, on a desk your programmer knows, with hot-swap spares on the truck, not as a stack of cases for the floor to sort out at call. Spec it as part of a full turnkey build and one crew runs the moving lights and the LED volume together, so the wall throws the motivated source and the movers fill the rest of the frame.

      That is the whole pitch: pick by the job, get a package that holds up on camera, and have it land on your stage ready to patch and go.

      Spec the rig
